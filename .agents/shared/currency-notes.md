# Currency notes

Names, schemes and editions that sources and older textbooks still use but that are retired. Shared by write-quizzes and write-flashcards. Read this before writing or reviewing, and use the current form. Mention the old name only as "formerly called" where it helps a learner; keep guideline titles as published.

When a lesson finds another retired term, add a row. When the current form here is itself superseded, change the row; don't keep both.

The batch gate (`gate` in both skills) warns on every match of a Gate entry in new or edited content, sources included. Each backticked entry is a case-insensitive regular expression matched as whole words, skipped right after "formerly" or "formerly called"; it can't contain `|`. Give a row Gate entries only when a phrase marks the retired form with few false matches; leave the cell empty for rules a phrase can't catch.

## Current editions

- Tumor classification: WHO Classification of Tumours, 5th edition, except Soft Tissue and Bone (6th edition, 2026; Hornick et al., Am J Surg Pathol 2026;50(12):e48-e64) and Digestive System (6th edition, 2026; Arends et al., Histopathology 2026, doi:10.1111/his.70116, PMC13128328; anal HSIL follows LAST). Check whether a site's 6th edition volume has appeared before writing.
- Staging: AJCC 8th edition, unless the site has a Version 9 chapter; check the site before writing a stage.
- Spleen, liver and kidney injury grades: AAST 2018 revision (Kozar et al., *J Trauma Acute Care Surg* 2018;85(6):1119-1122) adds CT vascular injury and active bleeding (grade IV within the organ, grade V beyond it). WSES 2017 splenic and liver trauma guidelines reprint the 1994 tables; name the edition on the card.
- Gynecologic cancer reviews: FIGO Cancer Report 2025 (*Int J Gynaecol Obstet* 2025;171 suppl 1) replaces the 2021 updates. It uses GLOBOCAN 2022 figures; cancer incidence and mortality come from GLOBOCAN 2024, so rankings can differ (cervical cancer is fifth in women worldwide in 2024, fourth in 2022). Name the GLOBOCAN edition behind a ranking.
- Contraceptive eligibility: WHO Medical Eligibility Criteria, 6th edition (November 2025). DMPA in breastfeeding women before 6 weeks postpartum is category 2 (was 3 in the 2015 5th edition); name the edition in the stem or on the card.
- HPV vaccine dose count: WHO SAGE recommends a single dose for girls aged 9 to 14 (October 2026); the PIDSP 2026 calendar still gives 2 doses, and no DOH issuance on the NIP dose count was found. Don't key a dose count for girls 9 to 14 without naming the schedule's body in the stem or on the card.
- Thiopurine pharmacogenetics: CPIC 2025 update (Maillard et al., *Clin Pharmacol Ther* 2026, doi:10.1002/cpt.70209) replaces the 2018 update (PMC6576267). TPMT or NUDT15 poor metabolizers with malignancy start at a 10-fold lower dose given thrice weekly; nonmalignant disease takes a non-thiopurine agent.
- Hepatic encephalopathy: ACG 2026 (*Am J Gastroenterol* 2026;121(3):588-618) replaces AASLD/EASL 2014.
- Methylmalonic and propionic acidemia: first revision (Forny et al., *J Inherit Metab Dis* 2021, doi:10.1002/jimd.12370) replaces the 2014 guideline.
- Phenylketonuria: European guideline first revision (van Wegberg et al., *Mol Genet Metab* 2025;145(2):109125) replaces the 2017 guideline.
- Wilson disease: EASL-ERN 2025 (doi:10.1016/j.jhep.2024.11.007) is newer than AASLD 2022; check it before keying treatment.
- Malignant hyperthermia triggers: all volatile anesthetics (sevoflurane, desflurane, isoflurane) and succinylcholine; ether and methoxyflurane are no longer in use.
- IMCI sick young infant: WHO *Management of the sick young infant aged up to 2 months* (2019) replaces the 2014 chart booklet young infant section. Fast breathing alone is pneumonia at 7 to 59 days (oral amoxicillin 7 days) and possible serious bacterial infection under 7 days; local bacterial infection takes amoxicillin 5 days; severe jaundice is yellow palms or soles. The 2014 booklet still holds for the sick child aged 2 months to 5 years.
- Pediatric HIV first-line ART: WHO *Updated Recommendations on HIV Clinical Management* (December 2025) makes abacavir + lamivudine + dolutegravir preferred for neonates too (dolutegravir every 48 hours in the first 2 weeks); the 2021 guideline limited dolutegravir to infants from 4 weeks and 3 kg.
- Childhood diarrhea zinc: WHO 2024 suggests 5 mg/day at any age; the 10 mg (under 6 months) and 20 mg schedule is IMCI 2014, and PhilHealth 2022-0026 still gives 20 mg. Name the body with the dose.
- Cervical screening: the USPSTF update is still a December 2024 draft, so USPSTF 2018 stands; HRSA/WPSI (January 2026) prefers hrHPV testing, including self-collection, at 30 to 65. Philippine PHEx 2021: Pap smear or VIA every 3 years, 21 to 65.
- Leptospirosis: PhilHealth Circular 2026-0008 points to the DOH *Guidelines for Leptospirosis in Hospitals* (2019), which replaces the 2010 CPG; no copy found yet. Check it before keying doses, MAT titer or prophylaxis tiers.

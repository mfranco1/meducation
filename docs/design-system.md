# Design system

Top-level screen entry uses a shared 170ms fade with a 6px upward settle and `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` easing. The header remains stationary and screen content stays interactive. Reduced-motion preference disables this transition. In-screen updates, including quiz question changes, are immediate.

The Results hero presents “Quiz complete” and the score inside a hollow circular ring. Its gray track uses `grey[300]`; its clockwise arc starts at 12 o’clock and uses the score color from `theme.palette.scoreRing`. The 100-unit SVG uses a 47-unit radius and 4-unit stroke. One elapsed-time animation drives the arc, integer counter, and color together. A two-part Hermite curve advances about 85% of the score in the first 65% of the timeline, then eases continuously to the final value. Duration scales from about 535ms at 1% to 1,300ms at 100%. The stroke blends smoothly from red through yellow and light green to green around 58–63%, 73–78%, and 88–93%. These colors are decorative cues; the numeric result remains authoritative. Zero has no arc, and invalid percentages safely display zero. The final score remains available as stable screen-reader text while changing visual digits are hidden from announcements. Reduced-motion preference shows the final number, ring, and color immediately.

The dashboard's active-subject carousel is user-controlled, never auto-advances, and uses labelled previous/next controls with wraparound navigation. Show those controls only when its cards overflow the visible responsive layout; otherwise present the cards as a static row. Its subject card uses native button semantics and visible focus treatment; reduced-motion navigation jumps directly to the next card.

The MUI theme owns semantic feedback tones for correct, incorrect, and answer-under-review surfaces, borders, and separators. Quiz choices, the feedback header, and Browse Answers use these tokens so a palette adjustment stays consistent without changing the written correctness and review signals. The pearl surface uses `primary.light`.

Meducation uses a light, warm autumn palette with `#b9511b` as its restrained primary accent, off-white surfaces, strong type hierarchy, soft borders, 14px radius cards, and visible native MUI focus states. Correctness always combines written feedback, an icon, and color. Immediate feedback uses a compact tinted status header followed by a neutral reading surface; long explanations should have a clear heading, generous line height, and a constrained readable measure rather than a fully tinted wall of text. The shared rich-content renderer presents canonical stems and rationales as semantic Markdown, sanitized HTML, images, and KaTeX math. Inline equations align with text; display math has readable spacing and long expressions scroll within their own bounded area on narrow screens. Math fonts load locally. Tables also scroll horizontally on narrow screens, and images use meaningful alt text, lazy loading, their natural aspect ratio, and a responsive bounded width. The highlighted answer choices communicate which selection was correct or incorrect, so do not repeat answer summaries in feedback. Render optional high-yield pearls as separate callouts. A correct-answer choice receives a short, local green-circle burst, while centered celebration overlays remain reserved for streak and perfect-test events. Both effects are decorative, pointer-transparent, and non-blocking: they do not move focus, obscure controls, or shift layout. They honor `prefers-reduced-motion` by omitting moving circles while preserving written and static correctness signals. Visual formatting must not change canonical Markdown semantics.

For dashboard and subject catalogs, use MUI wave skeletons that match card and score dimensions to reserve layout space. Use restrained warm shimmer contrast and disable animation when the user prefers reduced motion. Keep headings, navigation, and locally available statistics visible while content loads. Mark loading sections `aria-busy`, give each section an accessible loading label, and keep placeholders out of the focus order. Keep shimmer skeletons visible during automatic retry waits; they already communicate that content is loading. After retries stop, use an unframed message group with a short heading, muted explanation, and a text-style primary Retry or Reload content action. It can sit inside an existing card or directly within a section; do not add a new card, border, shadow, or illustration for failure. Keep the action keyboard accessible with a visible focus state and comfortable touch target. Map internal errors to learner-safe copy; technical status codes and raw exceptions belong in developer logs only. Do not show zero or empty states until that resource has loaded successfully.
